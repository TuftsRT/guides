# Data Visualization

Data visualization turns data into charts, maps, and dashboards so you can explore patterns, check your analysis, and share results with others. This section introduces common data visualization tools and links to tutorials for using them at Tufts.

## Choosing a Tool

The right tool depends on your data, your experience with programming, and how you plan to share your work.

- **Point-and-click tools** such as Tableau let you build charts and interactive dashboards without writing code. They are a good fit for exploring a new dataset quickly and for publishing dashboards that others can interact with.
- **Programming languages** such as Python (with libraries like Matplotlib, seaborn, and Plotly) and R (with ggplot2) give you full control over every detail of a figure. Because your figures are generated by code, they are easy to reproduce and update when your data changes. See the [Python](../ai/python/index.md) and [R](../ai/r/index.md) guides to get set up.
- **Spreadsheet software** such as Microsoft Excel and Google Sheets works well for quick, simple charts from small tables.
- **Geospatial tools** such as ArcGIS and QGIS are designed for maps and spatial data. See the [Tufts Geospatial](https://go.tufts.edu/geospatial) resources for more information.
